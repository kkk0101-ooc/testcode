# Endpoint Scanner results interpretation R4

## RQ3: component maps predict matched finite interventions

RQ3 was supported overall. Eleven of the twelve preregistered C4a families met every confirmatory condition across two models, two references, and three component families. The sole exception was the ViT-B routing/value family under the normalized-zero reference. Its association remained positive and Holm-significant, while its median correlation of 0.099565 fell 0.000435 below the prespecified 0.10 practical-effect threshold.

The evidence therefore supports the general RQ3 conclusion with one registered boundary exception. The frozen all-family omnibus C4a field remains `NOT_SUPPORTED` because its rule required 12/12 families; this machine decision records the strict universality criterion rather than an overall absence of predictive association.

## RQ4: spatial information beyond mandatory baselines

RQ4 was not supported. None of the four C4b families simultaneously exceeded total Gradient × Input, raw gradient, regional Taylor, and random-region rankings by the registered 0.05 margin. Endpoint ranking produced small positive median gains over random regions in all four families (0.0011–0.0107), but these gains remained below the practical threshold. Regional Taylor had higher median scores in every registered family.

## Paper-level conclusion

For the evaluated ViT-B/16 and DeiT-S/16 models, structured Endpoint component mass generally predicted matched finite activation effects. The full-resolution spatial ranking did not add the preregistered amount of information beyond the mandatory baselines.
