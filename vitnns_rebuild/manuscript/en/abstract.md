# Abstract

Vision Transformers (ViTs) represent an image as patch tokens and transform them through multi-head attention, multilayer perceptrons, and residual streams. Conventional activation maps describe the magnitude of an internal response but do not directly identify the input locations from which a specified response change originates. We extend the learning-image view of the Neural Network Scanner to ViTs. Path integration over the input assigns the change in an internal scalar or class logit to the original input coordinates. We distinguish module-own-response maps, fixed-clean-attention conditional Value-path attribution, and internal component maps of a complete class attribution. Real computations on a ViT-B/16 trace patch embedding, block-0 attention, the first residual addition, the block-0 MLP, the second residual addition, all 12 blocks, final normalization, CLS selection, and the classifier. Completeness and pixelwise residual additivity check the numerical correspondence.

On a frozen 500-image cohort, we evaluate a prespecified block-0/head-9 internal target with paired insertion and deletion interventions over 4x4 input cells. The image-level fidelity difference between NNS and a target-matched patch-token baseline with uniform within-patch allocation is positive for all 500 images, with a median of 2.3082 and a paired-median bootstrap 95% confidence interval of [2.1111, 2.4452]. At the native 14x14 patch scale, the median difference falls to zero, locating the primary effect at the finer evaluation scale. In an independent 50-image class-output comparison, NNS scores below ViT Grad-CAM and above attention rollout; its registered contrasts with full-model integrated gradients and raw CLS attention are not significant. Complete-cut analysis further shows that the absolute attention share increases by 0.1391 from shallow to deep blocks, whereas the MLP share decreases by 0.2492.

Learning images connect internal ViT responses, spatial rankings, and component allocation in a common input coordinate system. Across the evaluated settings, their empirical behavior varies with the target, spatial scale, and internal path; the broad spatial-stability gate fails and Grad-CAM has higher fidelity in the independent class-output comparison.

**Keywords:** Vision Transformer; Neural Network Scanner; learning image; internal attribution; integrated gradients; fidelity; component decomposition
