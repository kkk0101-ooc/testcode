# 7. Conclusion

We extend the NNS learning-image view to Vision Transformers and express changes in specified internal responses and class outputs in the original input coordinates. The method separates module-own responses, fixed-clean-attention conditional Value paths, and complete cuts of class contribution. Real computations span patch embedding, attention, the first residual addition, the MLP, the second residual addition, all transformer blocks, final normalization, CLS selection, and the classifier. Completeness and pixelwise checks at both residual additions verify the numerical correspondence between the maps and their targets.

For the prespecified block-0/head-9 target under 4x4-cell intervention, the fidelity difference between NNS and a target-matched patch-token baseline with uniform within-patch allocation is positive on all 500 images, with median 2.3082. The median falls to zero at native patch scale and is substantially smaller at three other layer/head coordinates. In the independent class-explainer comparison, NNS scores below Grad-CAM and above rollout, while its contrasts with full-model IG and raw CLS attention are not significant. Complete-cut analysis supports opposite depth trends in the absolute contribution shares of attention and MLP branches.

Learning images thus connect input location, internal response, intervention-based evaluation, and component accounting. The current evidence supports their fine-scale use for specified targets and a reconstructable view of internal ViT contribution. Spatial stability, transfer across targets, and general superiority over existing explainers remain open empirical questions.
